
## 2.1 What are network types?

**Definition**: Computer networks can be classified into different types based on their geographical coverage, ownership, purpose, and access.

The most common classification is based on geographical coverage — how large an area a network covers.

We'll study these in order:

1. PAN — Personal Area Network
    
2. LAN — Local Area Network
    
3. CAN — Campus Area Network
    
4. MAN — Metropolitan Area Network
    
5. WAN — Wide Area Network
    
6. Intranet, extranet and Internet — classification by access and purpose

Note that CAN has two common meanings in networking contexts. Here, it means Campus Area Network, not Controller Area Network, which is a different technology used in embedded systems and vehicles.


## 2.2 PAN — Personal Area Network

![Glorystar TWS, OWS, Headphones & Speaker Specialist](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/tTlZLIEOVlycZJpkPGvJ8mGZuwWIiEf88O_HLk0NsZD-iuq2JvsmpRBkonTMmOwvKiocKStnYld9uaOEiSot0J4Wr7R39lSEEI1Q0WDISX7qLI1SqnxXUHgoWBO5GPXUWsXijiwIZiH84xCfhN_k5Gr6NURzji7Bkb9nWlTKqlM?purpose=inline)

**Definition**: A Personal Area Network (PAN) is a network that connects devices around an individual over a short distance.

**Coverage**: Typically a few metres, depending on the technology.

Examples:

- Smartphone connected to Bluetooth earbuds.
    
- Laptop connected to a wireless mouse.
    
- Smartphone sharing its Internet connection through a personal hotspot.

**Common** **technologies**: Bluetooth, USB and other short-range communication technologies.

**Advantages**: Convenient, portable and usually easy to set up.

**Limitations**: Short range and limited capacity compared with many larger networks.

**Interview** **point**: Bluetooth is commonly used for PANs, but PAN is a network category, not a specific protocol.


## 2.3 LAN — Local Area Network

![How Modem, Router, and Switch work in a Real Office Setup | Ganesh Gundapuneni posted on the topic | LinkedIn](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/gVQx2KYVfk85LGAH6DlV0qHAkl-bg1f30tbRaFT-xNaZfptXEINL5R-PrWNDIrHEMmcr_m-cpU3lu1vBlkTGh8RDoZ0KsDnsGnRC1mI0tAQ25fj9Ez7GQIxLqrjhZerTjJ4qGatfi1Bg9WvPhSlP5hQ0LkgAdcyNxQaADP09M7A?purpose=inline)

**Definition**: A Local Area Network (LAN) connects devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, office, computer lab or building.

**Coverage**: A relatively small area, from a room to a building or nearby buildings, depending on the design.

Examples:

- Computers in your college computer lab.
    
- Devices connected to your home Wi-Fi.
    
- Office computers connected through Ethernet switches.

**Common** **technologies**: Ethernet and Wi-Fi.

Advantages:

- High data-transfer rates are commonly achievable.
    
- Resources such as printers and files can be shared.
    
- A privately managed LAN can offer strong control over access and security.
    

Limitations:

- Coverage is limited compared with a WAN.
    
- Setup, maintenance and security still require proper management.

### Wired LAN vs Wireless LAN

|Wired LAN|Wireless LAN|
|---|---|
|Commonly uses Ethernet cables.|Commonly uses Wi-Fi.|
|Often provides stable, predictable connections.|Allows mobility and easier device connectivity.|
|Requires physical cabling.|Uses radio communication and can experience interference.|

**Important**: Wi-Fi is not the same thing as the Internet. A device can connect to a Wi-Fi LAN even when the Internet connection is unavailable.


## 2.4 CAN — Campus Area Network

![University WiFi: How to Build a Campus-Wide Wireless Network | Technical Guides | Purple](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/GsYPFMfVnHd8sikS7990oTbPpcNmWd67FLPPT8IEApEicH5aTHwHMNegg9G3ugYvnQE0R4DwuCg34jmyP4SWuwutAqzp5IVbYAXbalrmBpdApg5LbAG69XKc9mNruGSRO-lf3WDA7fZNYrLOSWRe_67QVyBD8UrEUsEAmru95_I?purpose=inline)

**Definition**: A Campus Area Network (CAN) connects multiple LANs across a campus or a group of nearby buildings under a coordinated network infrastructure.

**Coverage**: A campus, university, hospital complex or business campus.

Examples:

- A university connecting its library, laboratories, administration and hostels.
    
- A hospital connecting different buildings and departments.
    

Advantages:

- Enables communication between multiple LANs.
    
- Supports centralized network management.
    
- Allows sharing of services across campus buildings.
    

Limitations:

- Requires planning, networking equipment and maintenance.
    
- Larger deployments may involve significant installation costs.
    

**Key** **distinction**: A LAN may serve one building, while a campus network connects multiple LANs across a campus. Their physical coverage can overlap; these terms are descriptive categories, not rigid distance limits.


## 2.5 MAN — Metropolitan Area Network

![SCCI Group | Technical Services Specialists](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/mT2Qu8uvqRj6LdP7NR7ErcFFVPZHrcVavwhEZNBc0W_4bq3qkQqp754ra88rsJloHXt0kfjpxCuUlHWBPjURVfMNJ7dNIF9xNCqK8_DgAEZ-d5dw5xGyKkv9g1gN35tDGLFh4w0d0PzRzCnl_8GS_xSWYpyVgNZUTig0KvaYOdM?purpose=inline)

**Definition**: A Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) connects multiple LANs or other networks across a city or metropolitan region.

**Coverage**: A city or a metropolitan area.

Examples:

- A municipal network connecting government offices across a city.
    
- A service provider's network connecting business locations across a metropolitan region.
    

Common technologies: Metro Ethernet and optical-fibre infrastructure.

Advantages:

- Connects networks spread across a city.
    
- Supports communication between geographically separated sites.
    
- Can provide high-capacity connectivity.
    

Limitations:

- More complex than a typical small LAN.
    
- May depend on telecommunications providers and specialised infrastructure.


## 2.6 WAN — Wide Area Network

![コンピュータネットワークとは何か？](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/y5oVAgHwRQ8DjwOvVvgHLx8rXHiltuGautz9RihC24GOafmCQRRE_GgUIKLWV5NqY2kieIhes21v6hiBw03PkCo-lx_U5Jv8lhpLzLh9k4urVXW1mxLrF-9u5ujQWbIeLdh_bGM1Cy2IgfKY6QQCEgQL2w0lGQPT88bj_Uxoz3s?purpose=inline)

**Definition**: A Wide Area Network (WAN) connects devices or smaller networks over large geographical areas, potentially spanning countries and continents.

**Coverage**: Regional, national or global.

Examples:

- A bank connecting branches across India.
    
- A company connecting offices in different countries.
    
- The Internet, which interconnects networks worldwide.
    

Common technologies: Leased lines, provider networks, long-distance fibre links, cellular networks and satellite links.

Advantages:

- Enables communication over long distances.
    
- Connects geographically distributed offices and systems.
    
- Supports centralised services across multiple locations.
    

Limitations:

- Can be more expensive and complex to operate.
    
- Performance depends on the paths, providers and technologies involved.
    
- Security and access controls are essential.
    

**Interview** **point**: The Internet is a global interconnection of networks, not simply one giant LAN or one centrally owned WAN. It is commonly described as a network of networks.


## 2.7 Compare PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN and WAN

|Feature|PAN|LAN|CAN|MAN|WAN|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Full form|Personal Area Network|Local Area Network|Campus Area Network|Metropolitan Area Network|Wide Area Network|
|Typical scope|Around a person|Home, office, building|Campus or complex|City or metro region|Regions, countries, continents|
|Example|Bluetooth earbuds|Home Wi-Fi|University network|City-wide network|Bank's nationwide network|
|Common technology|Bluetooth|Ethernet, Wi-Fi|Ethernet, fibre|Metro Ethernet, fibre|Provider and long-distance networks|
|Relative scale|Smallest|Small|Larger campus|Metropolitan|Largest|

The coverage categories are approximate. There is no universal distance threshold that perfectly separates every LAN, CAN and MAN.


## 2.8 Intranet vs Extranet vs Internet

These terms classify networks by their access and purpose rather than geographical coverage. A company's intranet, for example, can span multiple cities.

## Intranet

**Definition**: A private network or set of services intended for an organisation's authorised members.

**Example**: An employee portal containing internal documents, policies and company tools.

**Access**: Restricted to authorised users.


## Extranet

**Definition**: A controlled extension of an organisation's private network or services that grants selected external parties access to specific resources.

**Example**: A supplier portal through which approved vendors can view orders.

**Access**: Limited access for selected external users, such as suppliers or business partners.


## Internet

**Definition**: The global system of interconnected networks that communicate using the Internet Protocol suite.

**Example**: Accessing public websites, online services and email systems.

**Access**: Publicly reachable services and private services can both use Internet infrastructure; access still depends on permissions and security controls.


### Comparison

|Feature|Intranet|Extranet|Internet|
|---|---|---|---|
|Primary purpose|Internal organisational communication|Controlled collaboration with external parties|Global connectivity and services|
|Typical users|Employees or members|Authorised internal and external users|Internet users, subject to service permissions|
|Access|Restricted|Restricted and selectively shared|Global infrastructure; individual services may be restricted|
|Example|Internal employee portal|Supplier portal|Public website|

**Remember**: An intranet is not necessarily physically separate from the Internet. It can use Internet technologies while restricting access through authentication, permissions and network security.
